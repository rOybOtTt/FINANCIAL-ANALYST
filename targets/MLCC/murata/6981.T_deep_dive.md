## 1. Technical moat: why Murata wins the hard parts

- **What an MLCC is.** Hundreds of alternating layers of barium-titanate ceramic and nickel electrode, sintered into a chip as small as 0.25 x 0.125 mm (008004). Capacitance rises with more and thinner layers. The craft is making sub-micron dielectric layers without defects, at trillions of units a year.
- **Murata's edge is vertical integration.** It makes its own dielectric powder, its own sheet-casting and printing equipment, and its own stacking and firing lines. Competitors buy powder (Sakai Chemical holds ~25-28% of barium-titanate supply) [A]. In-house powder lets Murata reach the thinnest layers first, which is what high-capacitance AI-server parts need.
- **Where it shows.** About 45% share in AI-server MLCCs and about 50% in automotive [A], the two segments where qualification takes 12-24 months and failure is not tolerated.
- **Limits of the moat.**
  - SEMCO is within 5 points in AI-server share [A].
  - Chinese makers (Fenghua, Three-Circle) are moving up from commodity parts into high-cap parts.
  - Murata is pruning part numbers across nine MLCC series (last orders Mar-2028) [A]: a mix choice that cedes commodity volume.

## 4. Value chain

- **Upstream:** barium-titanate powder (in-house; Sakai Chemical, Fuji Titanium for others); nickel and copper electrode paste; palladium is minor since the move to base-metal electrodes; sintering furnaces (largely in-house).
- **Murata:** powder, sheet casting, printing, stacking, firing, termination, test. Plants are in Japan (Izumo, Fukui, Okayama), China (Wuxi), the Philippines and Thailand.
- **Downstream:** distributors (about a third of volume; the double-ordering risk sits here), EMS/ODMs (Foxconn, Quanta, Wiwynn for AI servers), handset OEMs (Apple, Samsung, Chinese Android), and auto Tier-1s (Bosch, Denso, Continental). Named-customer shares are not disclosed (gap).
- **Chokepoint:** high-cap, small-case-size MLCCs for GPU power delivery. Murata and SEMCO supply 80-85% of them [A].

## 6. Investment philosophy: how to own this

1. **The cycle call and the stock call differ.** The MLCC upcycle probably has 2-4 more quarters: orders are accelerating, Japanese price hikes are still to come, and supply cannot answer before 2H27. That favours the high-beta laggards for a trade (Taiyo Yuden, Yageo, Walsin, down 41-55% from their highs), not Murata at 31x mid-cycle EBITDA.
2. **Murata is the name to own through the next bust, bought at the right price.** It fell among the least in every past downturn (-30% to -64%, vs up to -89% for Taiyo Yuden) and recovered its high fastest after 2018. Its own-price backtest is best when bought 30% off the peak in mid-cycle pauses (2011, 2019, 2022: +32% to +111% over 24 months). It is worst in true busts (2001, 2007: -8% to -25%).
3. **Staged entry plan.**
   - ¥6,200 (Base exit value at a 15x re-rated band): first third.
   - ¥5,000 (Base exit value): second third.
   - ¥4,100 (PW present value): the rest.
   - Each stage requires capacitor book-to-bill above 1.0 at the time of buying.

## 8. What would make us wrong

- **A regime change in the multiple.** AI-server MLCCs become a contracted, utility-like stream with structurally higher trough margins. Then the 2016-2025 band is the wrong yardstick, and ~15-20x is the new normal.
- **A Japanese price-hike leg that lifts FY3/28 EBITDA toward ¥1tn.** Even then the own band caps value near ¥7,000.
- **Sector-level way to be wrong.** The whole MLCC cohort re-rates together as an "AI picks and shovels" group, as memory did in 2025-26. Murata then trades with SEMCO's 48x forward P/E, not its own history.
